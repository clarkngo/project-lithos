# The Synod

**Role:** Theological and political body that adjudicates the moral status of Lithoi remains and Resonance use. Speaks in doctrine.

## Structure

The Synod speaks in the passive voice and in questions that are not really questions. Certainty is considered vulgar among them.

What is established: they adjudicate. They hide. They offer peace as a ritual of suppression.

## Motivations

Control the meaning of the Lithoi dead. If marrow-glass is a relic, extraction is sacrilege. If it is fuel, extraction is industry. The Synod's power is the right to say which sentence is true in which room.

Their public face is moral custody. Their private fear is restoration: what full, undamped Lithosomatic Resonance was before the Aethel-Core trade hollowed it into industry.

## Relationship to the Culling

The Culling is the wound they claim to dress. Guilt is their native language. It does not follow that they want the dead to speak.

## Relationship to Resonance

Resonance is permitted only inside doctrine. The Synod's climactic offer in Book One's outline is explicit: seal the Vault forever, suppress Caelen's Resonance permanently through ritual, and receive protection and peace in exchange.

Peace, here, means a quiet that has been imposed on a body.

## Book Two–Three: the internal argument, forced into the open

<details>
<summary>Contains Book Two–Three outline spoilers</summary>

The eldest Custodian's tired doubt (Book One) and **Custodian Voss**'s hardline certainty (Book Two) represent the Synod's own unresolved argument with itself — Voss is a believer, not a villain, shaped by watching a bungled Shift kill her mother at eleven. She captures Seraphina to force a reading of a second Lithoi seal before the Guild can claim it, and her own secret arrangement with Director Kell exposes the exact hypocrisy she has spent her career condemning. Reassigned to the records vault afterward — a quiet institutional punishment rather than a trial.

Book Three gives the argument its first public hearing. **Custodian Maren**, a twenty-nine-year-old Custodian thrust into the acting Eldest's chair by accident, opens the Hall of Canon to testimony for the first time in three generations and writes the hall's first public finding: no register except in public and open to those it names; no containment without a hearing; publicly funded houses of treatment; no practice of the rite a crime. Adopted 22–19. It does not resolve the doctrine's three-generation argument — it only moves it, for the first time, into a room where it can be seen arguing with itself.
