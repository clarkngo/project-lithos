# Costs

A Shift that does not cost something visible on the page is a failed Shift. The system has four recurring bills. They stack. They accumulate across a life.

## Caloric debt

A Shift burns stored fat and muscle at a lethal rate. Hunger has a voice. Long-term users are visibly gaunt, ravenous, and cold-sensitive.

Caelen's early forest days already show the debt before he has a word for it: thinning wrists, a cold banked behind the ribs, food that cannot keep up with what the changed body spends.

## Tissue tearing

The body is not a suit. Form Vectors rewrite scaffold through living meat. Expect torn skin, cracked nails, loosened teeth, a voice gone raw. Healing, when it comes too fast or too warm, is not mercy — it is another sign the marrow is doing work the person did not order.

## Post-Shift hypothermia

Resonance work generates violent thermal debt. Ember-bright Shifts overshoot and then crash. Marsh Shifts leave a long wet chill. The brass conduit is cold before and scalding after; the meat is the opposite by the time the aftermath arrives.

Ambient cold and heat should be tracked in every scene that follows a Shift. The minutes it takes to remember a name are also the minutes the body is losing heat it cannot spare.

## Atavistic Drift

The psychological toll of repeated or prolonged Shifting: the mind's slow erosion toward predatory instinct and away from human identity.

Characters **drift**. They do not level up.

Drift is named and feared (not left as an abstract danger) once the people who love Caelen have seen him Shift on purpose. It is a meter across Book One: introduced in the first full Shift, tested when Rook pulls away, nearly lost in the Wastes, confronted when the Vault shows what undamped Resonance used to be.

## Aftercare (working practice)

Not doctrine — a field list for characters who want someone to come back:

1. Food immediately, more than seems decent.
2. Heat: dry clothes, a body, a fire that does not touch brass still cooling.
3. Time before speech. Do not demand a name in the first minutes.
4. Watch the eyes. Watch whether the person in them is still bargaining with the thing that just ran the bones.
