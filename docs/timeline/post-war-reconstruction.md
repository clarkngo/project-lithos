# Post-war reconstruction

"Reconstruction" here means the world the book walks through: not healed, only reorganized around the hole the Lithoi left.

The Culling is generations ago. The present is mines, doctrine, fringe towns, and badlands.

## What is established

- **The Mining Guild** owns deep-strata extraction, including the mine of Caelen's origin. Survey, blast, ledger.
- **The Warden Enforcers** police unlicensed Resonance and Aethel-Core trade.
- **The Synod** adjudicates the moral status of Lithoi remains and Resonance use.
- **Hollow Cut** — fringe settlement; found-family ground; destroyed in Act One's climax (outline).
- **The Glass Wastes** — over-mined badlands, contested enough that patrols thin out. Act Two's working ground.
- **The Vault** — sealed Lithoi repository, hidden even from the Guild according to Act Two intelligence. Contested.

## What reconstruction actually reconstructed

Industry, not the dead. Marrow-glass moved from atrocity to inventory. Resonance moved from whatever it was into a three-slot brass market. Peace, when the Synod offers it, is a lid.

The land is a wound. Reconstruction is the scar tissue that extraction learned to live on.

## Book Two–Three: from a single door to a public argument

<details>
<summary>Contains Book Two–Three outline spoilers</summary>

Roughly eight months after Book One's climax, Seraphina begins openly treating Drift with the tethering rite from a fixed camp at the edge of the Wastes — the series' first institution built on mercy rather than extraction, doctrine, or enforcement, and immediately a target for all three of the others. Book Two ends with the second seal closed on nobody's terms but the found family's own, and Varrick's death the story's first grief that no choosing can fix.

Book Three widens the argument from one settlement's mercy to the whole territory's doctrine. Chairman Reth's petition for a single enforceable standard forces the Synod's three-generation internal argument into public testimony for the first time, at the Hall of Canon in the Spire. The hall's resulting finding — public registers only, no containment without a hearing, funded houses of treatment, no practice of the rite a crime — does not end the argument. It only moves it somewhere it can no longer be settled quietly.
