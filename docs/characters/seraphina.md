## Voice / dialogue notes

Carries Spire-court formality as armor. Contractions vanish when she is performing status, and reappear — often mid-sentence — when she forgets to.

The slip is the characterization. Do not explain it; let it happen in the line.

She is caught between worlds in the same grammatical way Caelen is caught between bodies.

## Relationships

- **Caelen's crew** — encountered when a job goes wrong; she is smuggling something the Synod wants kept sealed.
- **The Synod** — antagonistic interest in her cargo and in what the Vault would mean if unsealed. By Book Two, Custodian Voss gives her an ultimatum over the spreading rite; by Book Three, Voss sends her the key to the reliquary's unread fragments as the closest thing to an apology she has.
- **The Spire / House Ilvane** — origin and exile. Her father, Lord Ilvane, resurfaces in Book Three with an offer: House recognition for the rite in exchange for a public lie about how Tomas died. She refuses, publicly, in the Hall of Canon.
- **Tomas** — her brother, dead since before Book One opens. Named aloud, by her and then by the House steward Halloran, for the first time in Book Three's hearing.
- **Fenn / Ilse / Odo** — the first patients and the first students of the tethering rite, introduced Book Two.

## Arc summary

<details>
<summary>Contains Book One–Three outline spoilers</summary>

Book One, Act Two: introduced when a job goes wrong. Her cargo is Vault intelligence — proof of a sealed Lithoi repository the Synod has hidden even from the Guild. Exile backstory surfaces later: what she needs from the Vault for herself, not just the cause (a tethering rite that might have saved Tomas). Spire-formality cracks fully in a private scene with Caelen. She stands with the found family at the climax; her personal want and the collective stakes do not resolve into a single clean motive.

Book Two: carries the rite out of private grief into a growing network of treated families, at real and escalating cost — Custodian Voss's ultimatum, a forced reading that nearly breaks her, capture and rescue. Finishes the book teaching two archivists (Ilse, Odo) so the rite no longer depends on one exhausted woman.

Book Three: summoned back to the Spire by her father as the rite's growing fame makes her a political asset. Refuses his offer to recognize the rite in exchange for a sanitized lie about Tomas's death, and says his name plainly before the Hall of Canon instead — the act that finally cracks House Ilvane's three-generation silence. Receives, afterward, a small stone marker in the Ilvane garden and a key to four hundred unread reliquary fragments from Voss.
