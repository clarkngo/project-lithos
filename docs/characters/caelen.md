# Caelen

**Role:** Awakened protagonist — the spine of Book One.

## Physical description

Confirmed on the page (Book One, Act One, early chapters):

- Found at nine, sealed for as long as he can remember in amber-dark marrow-glass in the deep strata of a Mining Guild excavation.
- Body marked by that enclosure: joints unused to length, a tendency to move low and fast on all fours when panic takes him.
- Palms knit too warm, too quickly after the climb out of the mine.
- Eyes that throw lantern-light back at night, cat-fashion — Caelen does not yet know this about himself when Varrick first sees it.
- Caloric debt shows early: thinning wrists, grey lips, a cold that banks behind the ribs even when the air warms.

## Voice / dialogue notes

Act One is tightly bound to Caelen's POV (child, then adolescent). Do not leak adult vocabulary or geopolitical hindsight into early chapters. He thinks in hunger, cold, and the shape of the people he loves.

Mining-community cadence applies around him even when he is silent: clipped, elliptical sentences; subjects dropped; silence as a valid answer. He does not apologize twice — and for a long stretch he does not speak at all.

Interiority is earned through physical detail, not named emotion.

## Relationships

- **Varrick** — not a rescuer. Finds Caelen in the forest above the mine and takes him in on transactional terms: a boy who survived a strata pocket is worth more alive and quiet than reported. The found-family bond is built afterward, not granted at first sight.
- **Rook** — first peer since the mine. Volatile, loyal under the bravado; the first real fault line in the found family when he sees Caelen Shift.
- **Malakar** — Warden investigator assigned the strata-child case; hunts Caelen through procedure rather than cartoon malice.
- **Seraphina** — Act Two. Exiled Spire noble whose formality and the slips in it mirror Caelen's own between-worlds state.

## Arc summary

<details>
<summary>Contains Book One outline spoilers</summary>

Not Lithoi by blood, but irreversibly changed by proximity to the stratum that held him. Act One is the loss of an assumed-safe world (the mine as cradle, then Hollow Cut as found family) and the first named taste of Atavistic Drift. Act Two is the adult that loss produces: a Wastes runner who manages caloric debt and Drift as daily discipline, and who, at the end of the book, keeps his dangerous, resonance-marked autonomy rather than accept the Synod's offer of a quiet, suppressed peace.

Innocence, once lost, stays lost. No clean victory.
