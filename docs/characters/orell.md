# Orell

**Role:** Warden captain — Malakar's former superior, later his closest ally.

## Physical description

Not described in detail on the page. Treat as an older, composed Warden officer — the kind of man whose dry pragmatic authority reads as competence rather than cruelty, until it's tested.

## Voice / dialogue notes

Flat, procedural, Warden cadence by training — the same clause-stacked grammar as Malakar's. Cracks, when it cracks, into plain unarmored grief rather than anger.

Loyalty, for him, is a thing proven by small actions long before it is ever said aloud.

## Relationships

- **Malakar** — assigns him the strata-child case in Book One (as his superior at Kesh Hollow). In Book Two, sent to investigate Malakar's sympathizer network; chooses, instead, to join it after learning what his own order did at Greyfen. By Book Three, his equal and closest partner.
- **The Warden order** — eleven years of service and silence, until he decides the silence was never neutral.
- **Rook** — Book Three. An unlikely friendship; the two of them end up running the camp's defenses together.

## Arc summary

<details>
<summary>Contains Book Two–Three outline spoilers</summary>

Introduced in Book One as Malakar's superior, assigning him the Long Seam case. Reappears in Book Two hunting Malakar's quiet sympathizer network — not to arrest him, but to ask whether the order he serves was ever the one he signed up for. Defects outright after learning a Warden containment operation killed a mother and son at Greyfen over an unlicensed rescue Shift; helps engineer the rescue at the second seal, at the cost of his own commission.

Book Three: testifies publicly alongside Malakar at the Spire hearing — the first time either of them has spoken as themselves rather than through doctrine. Runs the Warden house of treatment at Kesh Hollow by the book's end, teaching men who once wore his own uniform how to hold a working steady.
