# Custodian Voss

**Role:** The Synod's hardline answer to the eldest Custodian's admitted doubt — a believer, not a villain.

## Physical description

<!-- TODO: confirm against manuscript — age, build; how her bearing differs from the eldest Custodian's tired weight. -->

Younger than the eldest Custodian, her composure sharper and more honed rather than worn. Plain undyed Synod robe, as doctrine requires, carried with none of the elder's visible exhaustion — until Book Three, when four months in the records vault age her "the way a wall ages in weather."

## Voice / dialogue notes

Carries the Synod's established cadence — every statement bent toward a question that expects no real answer — but colder than the eldest Custodian's, stripped of her tired weariness. Genuinely believes her own doctrine; the style guide's rule against cartoon antagonists is load-bearing for her specifically.

## Relationships

- **Seraphina** — gives her an ultimatum in Book Two (stop the rite, or the Wardens will be told to treat every treated family as a confirmed unlicensed household) and later holds her captive to force a reading of the second seal.
- **Director Kell** — their off-the-books arrangement over the second seal is the hypocrisy that ends Voss's authority once exposed.
- **Custodian Maren** — her eventual successor as the Synod's authority on Resonance doctrine. Voss tells her, late in Book Three, what she wishes someone had told her at nineteen: that the question is never certainty versus doubt, but what you can bear to have been wrong about.

## Arc summary

<details>
<summary>Contains Book Two–Three outline spoilers</summary>

Introduced in Book Two as the Synod's hardline counterweight to the eldest Custodian — believes Book One's mercy toward Caelen will cost more lives than the old suppression doctrine ever did. Her personal history surfaces in a flagged POV chapter: at eleven, she watched a gentle man's bungled Shift kill three people, including her mother, in a garden. Captures Seraphina to force a reading of a second Lithoi seal before the Guild can reach it, in genuine (if compromised) service of preventing a worse industrial outcome; her secret arrangement with Director Kell to grant the Guild a licensed allowance is exposed during the rescue, ending her authority.

Book Three: reassigned to the Synod's records vault as a quiet institutional punishment. Gives Custodian Maren, her successor, the hardest-won piece of wisdom in the book — that doctrine and doubt are both just ways of avoiding the real question of what you can bear to be wrong about. Sends Seraphina the key to four hundred unread reliquary fragments by way of apology, the only kind she knows how to give.
