# Fenn

**Role:** Drift-afflicted brass-fitter — the mirror Book One's Vault chamber only let Caelen imagine.

## Physical description

Confirmed on the page (Book Two, Act One, first appearance):

- Younger than Caelen expected, close to Rook's own age. A faint permanent hitch has settled into his shoulders, as though mid-flinch from a blow that landed months ago and never fully stopped landing.
- Eyes hold a beat too long on anything that moves, then drag away with visible effort.
- Scarred, steady hands — a housing-fitter's trade, not a Resonant's by training.
- By Book Three, thinner, the hitch settled deeper into the base of his neck. Hands are the last part of him to go unsteady.

## Voice / dialogue notes

Dry, wry brass-worker's humor that survives even his own decline — a small mercy offered sideways, in Varrick's style rather than performed.

Speaks about his own condition plainly, without self-pity, in the arithmetic terms his trade taught him: costs, sums, what came out even and what didn't.

## Relationships

- **Caelen** — the first person Caelen ever anchors who isn't himself; the rite's mechanic (a banked, steady Resonant presence) only works because Caelen can hold still for someone else. Fenn tells him directly that the difference between them was luck, not courage.
- **Varrick** — bonds with him at the camp after Fenn chooses to stay rather than return home with nothing left to try. Present at Varrick's death.
- **Director Kell** — targets the camp directly once Fenn and Varrick are its only undefended members; Fenn Shifts alone, with no anchor, to protect Varrick, at permanent cost to his own remaining time.

## Arc summary

<details>
<summary>Contains Book Two–Three outline spoilers</summary>

Introduced walking three weeks to reach Seraphina's rite after Shifting unlicensed for years to pay for his mother's medicine — economic necessity, not recklessness or thrill. The rite slows his Drift but never cures it, exactly as promised; he stays at the camp afterward as its forge-keeper rather than return to nothing. Chooses, deliberately, to Shift without an anchor to protect Varrick from a Guild strike, burning through weeks of his remaining time in a single choice he never regrets.

Dies quietly in Book Three's first chapter — not spending himself on anyone's behalf, just finishing a housing at his bench, held steady by Caelen so he goes out as himself rather than as what the Drift would have made of him. Buried on the rise above the camp, near Varrick.
