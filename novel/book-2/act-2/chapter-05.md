# Chapter Five — What Was Taken

The eastern gate stood exactly as unlatched as Orell had promised, and Caelen understood, slipping through it into the annex's inner yard with Rook a half-step behind and Malakar bringing up the rear, that the easy part of the night had just, quietly, ended.

They moved low and fast through the gap between the second and third watch, the same careful discipline nine years had taught Caelen to hold even now, even with the coal behind his sternum already banked and ready, and found the cell block exactly where Orell's whispered directions had promised it — a low stone annex off the compound's northern wall, two guards posted at its single door, neither expecting trouble from a route command had never thought worth reinforcing properly.

Caelen took the first guard down with the same controlled measure he'd used at Fenn's fire, no more than the moment required, and Rook's knife-work handled the second before either man managed more than a single startled breath. They were inside before the alarm had any real chance of finding its voice.

---

Seraphina lay on a narrow cot in the third cell, and Caelen understood, seeing her, exactly how much three forced readings had actually cost her — thinner than she'd been even in the desperate flight from her own House nine years gone, her breath shallow, a tremor in her hands that had nothing to do with cold.

"Caelen." Her voice came out wrecked, disbelieving, and something in her composed, exhausted face broke entirely at the sight of him. "You shouldn't have come. Voss will—"

"Voss isn't getting the chance to finish that sentence," Caelen said, already working the crude lock with fingers gone briefly lengthened and precise, the small careful cost of a targeted half-Shift arriving and passing in the space of a breath. "Can you walk?"

"I can try." She was on her feet a moment later, unsteady, Rook's arm already there to catch her before she'd fully tested the standing, and Caelen watched something in her exhausted face settle, briefly, into pure relief at the simple fact of being touched by someone who wasn't trying to extract anything from her.

"The second seal," she said, urgent, even now, even exhausted past any reasonable limit. "It's not fully closed, Caelen. Three interrupted readings — each one left it a little more open than it was, and I don't know how stable that leaves it. Voss doesn't fully understand what she's asking me to do. Nobody's ever attempted this many partial readings on a Lithoi seal before. I don't know what happens if a fourth one gets forced before it's either finished properly or sealed back down."

Caelen felt the cold certainty of it settle into him alongside everything else the night had already cost. "Then we deal with that once you're safe," he said. "One debt at a time."

---

The alarm found them at the yard's edge, not through any failure of Orell's careful planning, but through the same flat bad luck that had cost them a clean approach at the eastern crossing weeks before — a guard on an unscheduled round, catching sight of four shapes where the night's roster accounted for none, his shout carrying fast and sharp across the compound's stone walls.

"Go," Malakar said, already turning to meet the guards converging from the barracks, his old Warden training arriving fast and certain despite eight months away from a proper command. "I'll hold the gate. Orell—"

"Already blown," Orell said, appearing from the shadow of the eastern wall with a blade already drawn, his composed face carrying the particular grim clarity of a man who had just watched his own careful three weeks of small delays collapse into a single irreversible night. "They'll have my absence from post flagged within the hour regardless of how this goes. I'm coming with you, Inquisitor, and I'd rather not spend the conversation about it arguing while men with crossbows close the distance."

They fought their way toward the gate in a chaos that Caelen would only fully reconstruct afterward, piece by piece — Malakar and Orell holding the narrow approach together with a coordination that spoke to eleven years of shared training even now, on opposite sides of a broken oath; Rook half-carrying Seraphina toward the wall's gap; Caelen himself putting down two more guards with a controlled measure that cost him a wave of cold and nothing worse, because nine years of discipline held even here, even now, even with everything at stake.

It was passing the compound's supply depot, in the last stretch before the gate, that Caelen saw the crates.

Guild-marked. A full dozen of them, stacked deep in the depot's open bay, provisions and equipment both, stamped with the same regional insignia Kell herself had worn at their own camp's fire weeks before.

"She's supplying this," Rook said, breathless, understanding arriving in his voice at the same moment it arrived in Caelen's own chest. "Kell. The whole operation. Voss and the Guild, working together, the entire time."

"Later," Malakar barked, over his shoulder, still holding the gate's narrow approach against three more guards closing fast. "Whatever it means, it means it later, once we're not standing in the middle of proving it with our own lives!"

---

They cleared the wall's gap with the last of the compound's alarm bells ringing wild behind them, and did not stop running until the annex's low grey silhouette had shrunk to nothing against a horizon just beginning, indifferently, to grey with actual dawn.

Seraphina collapsed the moment the adrenaline finally released her, and Caelen caught her before she hit the ground, feeling the full hollow weight of what three forced readings had actually spent of her.

"She needs rest," Malakar said, grim, blood at his shoulder from a blade that had come considerably closer than any of them would have preferred. "Real rest, days of it, which I don't believe we currently have the luxury of offering her."

"The seal's unstable," Caelen said, quiet, the weight of everything the night had cost settling into him alongside everything it had failed to resolve. "She told me, before the alarm. Three interrupted readings, and neither finished nor properly resealed. Voss isn't going to simply let that sit unattended, not with the Guild's own supply crates confirming exactly how much she's already spent building this operation."

"Then we haven't actually finished anything tonight," Orell said, quiet, still catching his breath, his own composure carrying the particular grim weight of a man reckoning with the full size of what his choosing had actually cost him. "We've only bought a delay. Voss will regroup. The Guild will press harder, now that their own arrangement's been seen by eyes it was never meant to reach. And that seal's still sitting out there, cracked open by three forced attempts, waiting for whoever reaches it next to finish the job properly or make it considerably worse in the trying."

Caelen looked back once, toward the annex's grey silhouette, toward a door he understood now would not stay closed simply because they had managed, at terrible cost, to pull one exhausted archivist free of it.

"Then we get her strong enough to walk," he said, "and we go back and finish what tonight only started. Not for Voss's deadline. Not for Kell's ledger. For whatever's actually sitting behind that seam, still unclosed, still waiting on somebody honest enough to close it properly instead of on someone else's terms."

He did not know, saying it, how many more costs a debt like that one still had left to collect. He only understood, looking at Seraphina's exhausted, breathing, entirely present face against his own shoulder, that whatever the second seal still owed the world, it was not going to be paid by leaving it half-open behind them.
