# A Note on the Territory — Book Two

*(Supplements, rather than replaces, [Book One's territory note](../../book-1/front-matter/map.md). This account covers only the ground this book adds north of the Glass Wastes — the country the found family had never, before now, had reason to cross.)*

Book One's account of this territory stopped at the Wastes' own contested edge, because that was as far as any of its business ever required going. This book's business does not stop there, and the ground north of it keeps its own record of what three generations of extraction, doctrine, and quiet institutional appetite have built on top of the Culling's original grief.

**The camp** does not appear on any survey, official or otherwise, and its keepers would prefer it stay that way — a fixed point rather than a settlement, built into a fold of the Wastes' own scarred hillside, close enough to contested ground that neither the Guild nor the Wardens have ever found it worth the trouble of watching closely. Ordinary country begins a few days' walk north of it, and the losing of the Wastes' particular protection is a cost this account does not believe the camp's keepers fully priced in until they'd already spent it.

**Underlow** sits at the losing edge of that ordinary country — a trading post and broker hub of the unglamorous, necessary kind, dealing honestly in salvage and considerably less honestly in the rumors that travel alongside it. Neither Guild nor Warden territory in any formal sense, though both keep half an eye on it regardless.

**Kesh Hollow** garrisons the Warden order's regional command, unchanged in purpose since Book One's account of it, though its current Inquisitor-Commander would find considerably less use for the old description of the post as merely a place orders were issued from. This book's business runs closer to its walls than any prior account dared, and finds, inside them, both the doctrine's continued cruelty and the first real cracks in its unanimous face.

**The old Synod annex** appears on no map at all, official or rumored, which was always the exact point of it. A decommissioned reliquary outpost by its own paperwork, and something considerably older and more carefully guarded underneath that paperwork — a second Lithoi seal, smaller than the first this account has record of, no less real for its modesty, and no less capable of costing everyone who reaches for it more than they budgeted to spend.

**The sympathizer waystation**, two days south of the annex, holds no official standing whatsoever — a gathering point built, informally, out of the same desperate need that first carried families to the camp's own gate. This account declines to mark its exact position, for reasons its own residents would consider self-evident.

*Whatever ground this book adds to the territory, one thing holds steady from Book One's account and every account since: no single ledger — Guild, Synod, Warden, or otherwise — has ever managed to map this country whole, and the closest anyone has come to an honest version of it has always been the one with the least left to gain from lying.*
