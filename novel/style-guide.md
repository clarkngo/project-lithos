# The Last Lithoi — Style Guide

This document governs prose voice, sensory discipline, dialogue cadence, and terminology for the manuscript. Every chapter should be checked against this guide before it is considered done.

---

## 1. Prose Voice

- **Third-person limited**, tightly bound to a single point-of-view character per chapter. Do not head-hop within a scene.
- **Past tense** throughout, no exceptions for flashback or dream sequences (use clear scene breaks instead of tense shifts).
- Sentence rhythm favors **short, declarative units under physical duress**, opening into longer, more lyrical sentences during rare moments of safety or memory. Let the *length* of the prose mirror the character's physiological state — panic clips sentences; exhaustion drags them; grief slows them to a crawl.
- Avoid throat-clearing openings ("It was a day like any other"). Enter scenes in motion, mid-sensation, or mid-consequence.
- Interiority is earned through physical detail, not stated emotion. Prefer *"his molars ached from clenching"* over *"he was anxious."*

## 2. Sensory Rules

The Lithoi world is felt through the body before it is understood through the mind. In every scene, ground the reader in at least three of the following five before moving to plot or dialogue:

1. **Temperature** — resonance work generates violent thermal debt; ambient cold/heat should always be tracked, especially post-Shift.
2. **Weight and pressure** — stone dust, ore-slick air, waterlogged clothing, the drag of an overloaded pack, the specific heaviness of a body not yet used to its own mass.
3. **Sound before sight** — in mines, forests, and Warden holds, let sound announce danger a beat before the eye confirms it (groaning timber, a Cadence's subsonic hum, wet breathing).
4. **Smell as memory trigger** — amber-sap, scorched marrow-glass, wet loam, brass oil, blood-iron. Smell should recur as an anchor for trauma and homesickness alike.
5. **Taste** — starvation and caloric debt are core to Resonance; characters should taste their own hunger (copper, bile, the phantom sweetness of Aethel-fume) as a recurring physiological marker.

**Hard rule:** Transformations are never rendered as clean or empowering in the moment. Every Shift costs something visible — torn skin, cracked nails, a tooth loosened, a voice gone raw. Do not let a Shift read as a costume change. Write the tearing. Write the cold that follows. Write the minutes it takes to remember your own name afterward.

## 3. Dialogue Cadence

- **Lithoi-adjacent mining communities** (Caelen's origin) speak in clipped, elliptical sentences — subjects dropped, verbs doing double duty. Silence is a valid answer. Nobody apologizes twice.
- **Warden Enforcers** speak in procedural, clause-stacked sentences — the grammar of people who have learned that precise language is what keeps them blameless later.
- **The Synod** speaks in the passive voice and in questions that are not really questions. Certainty is considered vulgar among them.
- **Varrick** (veteran rogue mentor) undercuts tension with dry, economical humor — never whimsy. His jokes are load-bearing; they exist to keep him from saying the true thing.
- **Rook** speaks fast, interrupts himself, and uses profanity as punctuation rather than emphasis. His cadence loosens — slows, softens — only in scenes of found-family intimacy, and the shift should be visible on the page.
- **Malakar** speaks with clinical precision that frays, almost imperceptibly, whenever doctrine and conscience collide — a stalled clause, a word chosen and then replaced.
- **Seraphina** carries Spire-court formality as armor; contractions vanish when she is performing status, and reappear — often mid-sentence — when she forgets to.
- Avoid dialogue tags beyond *said/asked*. Let cadence and action beats carry characterization instead of adverbs.

## 4. Point of View & Chapter Discipline

- Act One is anchored almost entirely in **Caelen**, child and then adolescent, to preserve the innocence-lost arc. Any deviation must be flagged in the outline before drafting.
- Do not let adult vocabulary or hindsight leak into Caelen's early POV. A child does not think in the language of geopolitics or magic theory — he thinks in hunger, cold, and the shape of the people he loves.
- Chapter openings ground the reader in place and body within the first two sentences. Chapter endings land on an image or a single sensory beat, not a summary.

## 5. The Lithosomatic Resonance System (Terminology Lexicon)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Lithoi** | The extinct humanoid-terrene lineage whose dense marrow conducted planetary resonance. Their remains are the source of all Aethel-Cores. Treated with a mixture of reverence, grief, and industrial indifference depending on faction. |
| **Aethel-Core** | Vitrified, fossilized Lithoi marrow and organ tissue, mined from deep strata and cut into conduit-ready shards. The base fuel of all Resonance work. |
| **Lithosomatic Resonance** | The discipline/magic system: locking up to three Aethel-Cores into a brass arm conduit to trigger a physical transformation ("a Shift"). |
| **Form Vector** | The first conduit slot — determines the skeletal scaffold the body reshapes toward (e.g., Talon Vector, Barrow Vector). |
| **Affinity Cadence** | The second conduit slot — the elemental breath/temperament layered onto the Form (e.g., Ember Cadence, Marsh Cadence). |
| **Mutator Node** | The third, optional conduit slot — a modifier that scales or distorts the combined Form and Cadence (e.g., Gross Node for mass, Feral Node for instinct-dominance). |
| **Conduit** (brass arm conduit) | The mechanical housing, typically forearm-mounted, that locks Aethel-Cores in place and channels Resonance into the body. Always brass, always cold before a Shift and scalding after. |
| **The Shift** | The act and event of transformation. Never instantaneous in prose — always rendered as a violent biological process with a beginning, middle, and aftermath. |
| **Atavistic Drift** | The psychological toll of repeated or prolonged Shifting — the mind's slow erosion toward predatory instinct and away from human identity. Characters "drift"; they do not "level up." |
| **Caloric Debt** | The starvation cost of Resonance work — a Shift burns stored fat and muscle at a lethal rate. Long-term users are visibly gaunt, ravenous, cold-sensitive. |
| **Marrow-glass** | Colloquial term for raw, uncut Aethel-Core material as it's pulled from the strata — glassy, amber-dark, faintly warm to the touch. |
| **The Culling** | The historical genocide/extraction event in which the Lithoi were hunted to extinction for their marrow. Pre-dates the novel's present by generations; its guilt structures every faction. |
| **The Warden Enforcers** | The paramilitary order that polices unlicensed Resonance use and Aethel-Core trade. Procedural, bureaucratic violence. |
| **The Mining Guild** | The industrial body that owns and operates the deep strata extraction sites, including the mine of Caelen's origin. |
| **The Synod** | The theological/political body that adjudicates the moral status of Lithoi remains and Resonance use. Speaks in doctrine. |
| **The Glass Wastes** | The scarred, over-mined badlands of Act Two — a landscape hollowed out by generations of extraction. |
| **The Vault** | A major Act Two location — a sealed Lithoi repository, contested by the Guild, the Synod, and independent actors. |
| **Deep strata** | The lowest, oldest mining levels, where the richest and most dangerous marrow-glass is found — and where Chapter One's blast occurs. |
| **Record-Core** | A rare marrow-glass shard vitrified as memory rather than fuel — a full accounting laid down by Lithoi hands before the Culling. Pulses in the presence of someone with a strong Lithoi connection rather than radiating even warmth like an ordinary Aethel-Core. |
| **The tethering rite** | A Lithoi-era methodology, sealed in the Synod's reliquary, for drawing a drifting mind back from the edge of losing itself — not a cure, but a treatment the Synod has never offered to families who lost someone to Drift. |
| **Custodian** | A Synod rank/title, not a single named office — Synod representatives speak in the order's characteristic question-shaped cadence (see §3) regardless of which individual holds the title. |
| **Director** | A senior Mining Guild rank, above ordinary survey factors and crew bosses — authorized to commit expedition-scale resources and to speak for the Guild's ledger directly. |

## 6. Naming Conventions

- Human/settler names are consonant-heavy and clipped (Caelen, Varrick, Rook, Malakar, Seraphina) — no apostrophe-fantasy names, no invented diacritics.
- Lithoi-origin terms favor soft, resonant sounds (Aethel, marrow-glass) to contrast against the hard consonants of the human world that consumes them — the naming itself should carry the novel's central grief.
- Faction names are always capitalized as proper nouns (the Guild, the Synod, the Wardens) and never abbreviated in prose, only in dialogue by characters who work within them.

## 7. Things to Never Do

- Never let a Shift resolve without cost paid on the page.
- Never explain the magic system in an infodump; reveal it only through what a body endures.
- Never let dialogue state a character's backstory outright — let it surface through cadence, omission, and physical tells.
- Never use "suddenly" — earn the surprise through pacing instead.
- Never resolve Act One's losses with a clean victory. Innocence, once lost, stays lost.
