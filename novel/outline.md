# The Last Lithoi — Book One: Full Outline

**Structure:** Two-act structure spanning a single protagonist's life — a childhood arc (Act One) and the adult arc it produces (Act Two). See [style-guide.md](style-guide.md) for voice and terminology governing every beat below.

**Protagonist:** Caelen — found as a child sealed in amber-dark marrow-glass in the deep strata of a Mining Guild excavation. Not Lithoi by blood, but irreversibly changed by proximity to the stratum that held him. His arc is the spine of both acts: the loss of an assumed-safe world, the found family that replaces it, and the choice, at the end of Act Two, to keep his dangerous, resonance-marked autonomy rather than accept the Synod's offer of a quiet, suppressed peace.

---

## ACT ONE — The Childhood Arc

*POV: Caelen, ages 9–16. Anchors the innocence-lost throughline. See style-guide §4 for POV discipline.*

### Act 1 beats (chapter-by-chapter)

1. **Chapter 01 — "What the Deep Struck"**
   The Guild's deep-strata excavation blast goes wrong. Nine-year-old Caelen is pulled screaming from an amber-encased stratum pocket no survey team charted — his body already changed by whatever slept beside him down there. Violent awakening, mine collapse, and a raw, animal escape upward through flooding shafts into the wet forest above. Ends on the treeline, alone, with the mine sealed (or seemingly dead) behind him.

2. **Chapter 02 — "Green Dark"**
   Caelen, injured and feral with cold and caloric debt, survives his first days in the wild forest above the mine. Introduces the physical grammar of his changed body (the world tastes wrong, sounds arrive too early, his hands ache in a shape that isn't quite his own). He is found — not rescued, *found*, the distinction mattering — by Varrick, a Guild-exiled scavenger working the forest's edge for salvageable marrow-glass.

3. **Chapter 03 — "The Terms of Taking In"**
   Varrick doesn't adopt Caelen out of kindness; he takes him in because a boy who survived a strata pocket is worth more alive and quiet than reported. Establishes Varrick's dry, load-bearing humor (style-guide §3) and the transactional shape of their early bond. Caelen's first attempted Shift — accidental, terrifying, unexplained — burns through a day's food in seconds.

4. **Chapter 04 — "Names for What He Is"**
   Varrick teaches Caelen the vocabulary he's missing: Aethel-Cores, conduits, the Warden Enforcers who'd confiscate or kill a "wild" resonant child on sight. First real explanation of Lithosomatic Resonance, delivered as survival instruction, not magic-school lecture (style-guide §7). Caelen learns the word for what happened to him: he wasn't touched by Resonance — he was *grown* into it.

5. **Chapter 05 — "Rook"**
   Introduce Rook, a street-hardened companion Varrick trades with in the fringe settlement of Hollow Cut. Rook is Caelen's first peer relationship since the mine — volatile, funny, fiercely loyal under the bravado. The three of them fall into an uneasy, found-family rhythm. First glimpse of Guild patrols actively searching for "the strata child" — the Guild wants to know what it lost.

6. **Chapter 06 — "The Mining Guild's Ledger"**
   A Guild survey crew corners Varrick's group over unlicensed marrow-glass trade. Caelen Shifts under duress for the first time on the page in full — the chapter that fulfills style-guide's hard rule on cost: torn skin, cracked nails, hypothermic aftermath, and the first true taste of Atavistic Drift pulling at his mind mid-fight. They escape, but the Guild now has a name and a description.

7. **Chapter 07 — "What Rook Knew"**
   Aftermath and fracture. Rook, frightened by what he saw Caelen become, pulls away — the found family's first real fault line. Varrick forces a hard conversation about what Caelen is becoming and what it will cost all three of them if he isn't taught control. Introduces the idea of Atavistic Drift as a named, feared condition rather than an abstract danger.

8. **Chapter 08 — "The Warden's Interest"**
   Malakar, a Warden Enforcer investigator, is assigned the strata-child case. First appearance from a partial outside POV insert (a controlled, clearly-marked exception to style-guide §4) establishing him as procedural, not cartoonish — a man doing violence through paperwork and doctrine. He begins closing in on Hollow Cut.

9. **Chapter 09 — "Hollow Cut Burns"**
   The Act One climax. Warden Enforcers raid Hollow Cut under Malakar's authority to seize Caelen. Rook returns in the crisis, found family reforming under fire rather than resolving cleanly (style-guide §7: no clean victories). The settlement is destroyed. Someone is lost — a settlement elder or an early ally who has been quietly protecting them — cementing the cost of Caelen's existence.

10. **Chapter 10 — "The Wastes Ahead"**
    Caelen, Varrick, and Rook flee Hollow Cut's ruins toward the Glass Wastes, the only territory Guild and Wardens contest too fiercely to patrol closely. Act One closes on the image of the child from Chapter One fully gone — Caelen is leaner, harder, marked — stepping into adult territory both literally and narratively. Bridges directly into the front matter and Act Two.

## ACT TWO — The Adult Arc

*POV: Caelen (adult, years later), with periodic controlled insert chapters from Malakar and Seraphina as the political stakes widen. See style-guide §4 — any POV deviation from Caelen must be flagged and deliberate, matching the exceptions established in Chapters 08 and continued here.*

### Act 2 beats (chapter-by-chapter)

1. **Chapter 01 — "The Glass Wastes"**
   Time jump. Adult Caelen operates as a hardened Wastes runner alongside Varrick (aged, slower, still dry) and a returned Rook. Establish the new status quo: caloric debt and Drift management as daily discipline, not crisis. The Wastes itself as a wound in the land — generations of extraction laid bare.

2. **Chapter 02 — "Seraphina of the Spire"**
   Introduce Seraphina, an exiled Spire noble, encountered when a job goes wrong and she's revealed to be smuggling something the Synod wants kept sealed. Her formal cadence and the moments it slips (style-guide §3) mark her as caught between worlds, mirroring Caelen's own.

3. **Chapter 03 — "What the Synod Wants Sealed"**
   Seraphina's cargo is revealed to be Vault intelligence — proof of the location of a sealed Lithoi repository the Synod has hidden even from the Guild. Caelen's crew is pulled into a job far bigger than salvage. First real articulation of the novel's philosophical stakes: the Synod's offer of "peace" versus the danger of what the Vault might restore.

4. **Chapter 04 — "Malakar, Still Hunting"**
   Malakar returns, now senior within the Wardens, still assigned to Resonance cases — his doctrine fraying further (style-guide §3) as he's ordered to suppress Vault knowledge rather than enforce law evenly. His conflicted POV insert deepens him from Act One's procedural threat into a man questioning his order's purpose.

5. **Chapter 05 — "The Toll of the Wastes"**
   A Wastes crossing forces Caelen into consecutive Shifts, pushing Atavistic Drift further than it's gone since Chapter 06 of Act One. Rook, now an adult who stayed rather than one who fled, has to talk Caelen back from the edge — the found-family bond tested and, this time, holding. Direct callback to Chapter 07 of Act One.

6. **Chapter 06 — "The Inquisitor's Alliance"**
   Malakar, disillusioned, defects from open Warden pursuit to a wary, transactional alliance with Caelen's crew — echoing Varrick's own transactional taking-in of Caelen in Act One Chapter 03. Trust is not given; it's negotiated, term by term, in clipped procedural cadence giving way, barely, to something honest.

7. **Chapter 07 — "Seraphina's Price"**
   Seraphina's exile backstory surfaces — why the Spire cast her out, and what she needs from the Vault for herself, not just the cause. Tension between personal want and collective stakes. Her Spire-formality cracks fully in a private scene with Caelen, contractions and all (style-guide §3).

8. **Chapter 08 — "The Guild Moves"**
   The Mining Guild, sensing the Synod and Wardens are distracted, moves to seize the Vault for extraction rights — reframing the antagonist pressure from a single hunt (Act One) to a three-faction scramble. Raises the stakes: the Vault isn't just knowledge, it's an untapped Aethel-Core reserve that could restart mass extraction.

9. **Chapter 09 — "The Vault"**
   The crew — Caelen, Varrick, Rook, Seraphina, and an uneasy Malakar — reach the Vault ahead of the Guild. Discovery of what's actually sealed inside: not just marrow-glass, but the preserved truth of the Culling and, implicitly, what full, undamped Lithosomatic Resonance was before the Aethel-Core trade hollowed it into industry. Caelen confronts what he could become if he let Drift win entirely — and what he'd be giving up of himself to do it.

10. **Chapter 10 — "The Synod's Peace"**
    The Synod arrives with an offer: seal the Vault forever, suppress Caelen's Resonance permanently through Synod ritual, and receive protection and peace in exchange. Full articulation of the novel's central philosophical choice.

11. **Chapter 11 — "What Caelen Chooses"**
    Climax. Caelen refuses the Synod's enforced peace, choosing dangerous autonomy — his own hard-won, Drift-shadowed self — over suppression. The found family (Varrick, Rook, Seraphina, and a Malakar who has to decide which side of his doctrine he actually believes) stands with him against both Guild and Synod pressure. No clean victory (style-guide §7): the Vault's fate, the cost to the crew, and Caelen's long-term relationship with Drift remain open wounds, not solved problems.

12. **Chapter 12 — "The Glass Wastes, After"**
    Denouement. A quieter chapter mirroring Act One's closing image — Caelen, marked and changed again, looking out over a landscape that hasn't been healed, only chosen. Sets up Book Two's stakes without resolving Book One's grief.

## Cross-Act Throughlines to Track

- **Atavistic Drift** as an escalating meter: introduced Act One Ch. 06, tested Ch. 07, nearly lost Act Two Ch. 05, confronted directly Act Two Ch. 09.
- **Found family before biological family**: Varrick and Rook function as Caelen's real kin from Act One Ch. 03 onward; Seraphina and (eventually) Malakar are folded in during Act Two — never presented as a replacement for loss, only as what was built after it.
- **The Culling's guilt** underwrites every faction's behavior (Guild extraction, Synod suppression, Warden enforcement) and should surface in dialogue and setting detail well before it's stated outright in Act Two Ch. 09.
- **Caelen's body as the site of the novel's argument**: every Shift chapter should cost something visible and cumulative — track scars, missing weight, and Drift markers chapter to chapter so Act Two's climax reads as earned, not sudden.
