# Chapter Eight — The Warden's Interest

*A note on point of view: this chapter departs, deliberately, from Caelen's perspective — the only such departure in Act One. See [style-guide.md §4](../../style-guide.md) for the discipline governing this exception.*

---

**MALAKAR**

The report sat on Inquisitor Malakar's desk for six days before he was permitted to open it, and he had learned, over eleven years in the Warden order, that the waiting was never accidental. Six days was long enough for a case to cool past urgency and short enough to still matter. Six days meant someone above him had already decided the shape of the conclusion, and was merely waiting for the paperwork to catch up to it.

He opened it on the seventh morning, in the cold north room of the Kesh Hollow garrison, with the shutters latched against a wind that found its way in regardless, and read it twice before permitting himself to form an opinion.

*Long Seam Collapse, Deep Strata Sublevel 4. Presumed juvenile survivor, unaccounted for remains, recovery status: unresolved, nine days elapsed. Subsequent incident, disused weigh-station, cart road east of Hollow Cut: unlicensed marrow-glass transaction interrupted by Guild recovery crew; one crew member deceased at scene, cause consistent with blunt trauma exceeding standard human force parameters; two crew members incapacitated, testimony concurrent regarding subject's apparent age (juvenile), apparent method (unassisted transformation, no conduit or housing recovered at scene, no core residue detected on subject's person per surviving witness account).*

Malakar read *unassisted transformation* a third time, and set the page down, and did not immediately pick up the next.

He was a precise man by training and, he had long since stopped pretending otherwise to himself, by temperament as well — the two reinforcing each other so thoroughly across a decade of service that he could no longer say with confidence which had produced the other. Precision was the Warden order's whole defense against the accusation, leveled at it constantly and not always unfairly, that its violence was simply violence wearing a uniform. A Warden who filed his reports in exact, clause-stacked language, who documented every force parameter and every witness discrepancy and every procedural justification before he ever laid a hand on a subject, could stand before the Synod's own tribunal afterward and say, truthfully, *I did only and exactly what the doctrine permitted, no more.* It was, Malakar had come to believe somewhere in his fourth or fifth year, the only kind of clean conscience the work actually allowed a man to keep. He had built his whole career on the keeping of it.

*Unassisted transformation* was not a phrase the doctrine had ever needed to account for before. There was no clause for it. There was no procedure that began with a subject who required no core and no housing to Shift, because the entire architecture of Warden law — confiscate the core, seize the housing, license the conduit or destroy it — assumed a Resonant was a man who had *chosen* his power and *purchased* the means of it. The doctrine had no language for a boy who simply *was* the thing other men spent a fortune and years of training to briefly wear.

He found, turning the page, that this absence of applicable clause troubled him considerably more than the dead man at the weigh-station did.

---

Captain Orell found him still at the desk past midday, papers arranged in the exact, squared-off order Malakar always kept them, and did not bother with pleasantries, which was one of the few things Malakar had ever genuinely respected in the man.

"You've read it."

"I've read it."

"And?"

Malakar considered the question with the same care he gave every question that mattered, which was to say he did not answer it quickly. "The doctrine provides no clean procedure for an unlicensed Resonant possessing no recoverable core," he said. "Which means whatever action we take will be built afterward to justify itself, rather than the reverse. I want that noted plainly before I'm asked to sign anything."

"Noted." Orell did not sound troubled by the distinction, which did not surprise Malakar; very few men were, once they'd been in the order long enough. "You're being assigned the case regardless. Command wants it closed before the Guild starts making its own arrangements to close it for us, and you're the driest hand we've got for something this delicate. No offense meant."

"None taken." It was, in fact, the precise truth about himself that Malakar least objected to hearing aloud.

"Guild's furious," Orell went on, dropping into the chair across the desk without invitation. "Lost a survey man outright, got two more laid up, and their own ledger's screaming that the collapse write-off doesn't square unless the boy's alive and unaccounted for. They want him back for study before anything else. We want him — " Orell paused, and Malakar noted the pause the way he noted every hesitation in every voice he ever listened to, a habit that had made him very good at his work and, he suspected, not an easier man to be around for it. "We want him contained," Orell finished, "before the how of that matters to anyone above your pay grade or mine."

"Contained," Malakar repeated. He turned the word the way he turned every word the order handed him to carry out, testing its edges for the places doctrine and plain fact might not sit flush against each other. "The witness reports describe a child. Nine years old, by the collapse date. Whatever it's become since, it began as a child pulled alive out of a mountain that should, by every account on record, have killed it outright."

"Wouldn't get too attached to the word *child*, Inquisitor," Orell said, not unkindly, rising. "Man at the weigh-station is dead regardless of what age the thing that killed him happens to answer to. The Guild's ledger doesn't note ages. Neither, generally speaking, does ours."

He left before Malakar could decide whether that last sentence had been offered as instruction or as something closer to an apology, and Malakar sat a long while afterward in the cold north room, the shutters still admitting their thin persistent wind, turning the phrase *contained before the how of that matters* over in the same precise, cataloguing part of his mind that had, across eleven years, learned to file away every sentence that troubled him a little more than the doctrine strictly required.

---

He worked the trail the way he worked every trail: patiently, and on paper first, because paper rarely lied to him the way frightened witnesses did.

Two more reports crossed his desk within the week. A Guild factor near Hollow Cut, questioned over irregular marrow-glass weights moving through the settlement's fringe market, had given up — under no duress Malakar's own methods would have countenanced, which he noted with the small clinical satisfaction of a man confirming his colleagues had, for once, kept within procedure — the name of a scavenger long known to Warden records for minor, low-priority trafficking. *Varrick,* the file read, no family name attached, a man who had spent two decades staying exactly interesting enough to track and exactly unimportant enough not to bother tracking closely.

A boy traveled with him now, the factor's statement said. Quiet. Watchful. Did not speak much, and when he did, spoke like someone relearning a language he'd once known and lost.

Malakar read that detail three times, and felt the stalled clause happen in his own throat before he identified it as such — a sentence he had begun forming, silently, about the shape of a childhood spent nine years sealed in stone before it was permitted to speak at all, and a second sentence, doctrinal, procedural, that overwrote the first before it could fully form: *subject's development is not relevant to threat assessment.*

He wrote the overwriting sentence down in his own case notes, in his own precise hand, because precision required him to record his own reasoning as thoroughly as anyone else's, and did not examine too closely why the pen hesitated, very slightly, at the second word.

He closed the file. Outside the latched shutters, the wind had not eased, and somewhere south and east of Kesh Hollow, past two days' ride through country he had crossed a dozen times before on considerably smaller matters, a fringe settlement called Hollow Cut sat unaware that its name had just been entered, in careful, clause-stacked language, into a Warden Inquisitor's traveling orders.

Malakar began, without particular haste, because haste was not a quality the doctrine had ever rewarded in him, to pack.
