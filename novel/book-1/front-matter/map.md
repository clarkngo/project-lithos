# A Note on the Territory

*(Intended to face the endpaper map. Reads as a cartographer's or archivist's annotation — in-world framing for whatever map illustration accompanies the printed or digital edition. See `apps/codex-web` for the interactive version.)*

No single survey has ever mapped this stretch of country whole, and the Guild, the Wardens, and the Synod each keep their own version for their own reasons, none of which agree with each other past the largest rivers. What follows is closer to the truth than any one of theirs, if only because it has nothing left to gain by lying.

**The deep strata** run beneath the northern range in seams older than any settlement built above them, worked by the Mining Guild for longer than living memory holds an honest account of. Long Seam, and the collapse that opened it wider than any survey charted, sits deepest of all the sublevels still worked. What woke there did not appear on any prior map, and does not appear on the Guild's official one now either.

**Hollow Cut** sat in the fold of a hillside south and east of the deep strata, a fringe settlement built from the Guild's discards, living off salvage and unlicensed trade and the particular stubbornness of people the larger world had already written off. It burned once, in the reckoning of this account, and rebuilt itself after — smaller, warier, with one forge at its quiet end that never reopened.

**Kesh Hollow** garrisons the Warden order's regional command, a cold stone hold north of Hollow Cut from which Inquisitors are dispatched to enforce recovery orders, licensing law, and the Synod's occasional doctrinal request dressed up as a suggestion.

**The Glass Wastes** spread wide across the continent's hollowed center — three generations of extraction fire and over-mining fused into scarred, glassy plate, poisoned in patches, silent in ways the living forest never is. Neither the Guild nor the Wardens commit real numbers to patrolling it, each too wary of the other to risk the ground uncontested, which makes it, perversely, the safest unowned country left.

**The Spire** rises somewhere east of the Wastes, seat of the old noble houses and, uneasily, of the Synod's central halls — a place of formal rooms and formal grammar, where exile is a sentence handed down quietly enough that most of the Spire never learns it happened at all.

**The Vault** is marked on no official survey whatsoever. Its rough location survives only in fragments — a smuggled record, a half-remembered doctrine citation, the kind of knowledge that gets people killed for carrying it rather than for using it. Where the Wastes run out and the old range resumes, somewhere in that seam, is as close as this account is willing to commit to paper.
