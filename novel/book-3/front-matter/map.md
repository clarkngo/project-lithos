# A Note on the Territory — Book Three

*(Supplements [Book One's](../../book-1/front-matter/map.md) and [Book Two's](../../book-2/front-matter/map.md) territory notes. This account covers the country this book adds: the Spire, and the road to it.)*

For two books the found family kept to ground nobody wanted badly enough to watch. This account concerns ground everybody watches, and the difference is the whole of the book.

**The Spire road** runs north and east out of the contested edge, through country that has never been mined to the bone: hedged fields, tidy villages, bakeries that hand a stranger a loaf without being asked. It takes six days to walk. It is the first road in this account on which a traveler is recognized before he is understood.

**The Spire** is a city built upward, ring on ring of pale stone, climbing a single white needle into the cloud. No smoke hangs over it. It stands clean as a tooth, and nothing in it shows what it stands on. Its old Houses survive by never becoming the subject of a story.

**The lower ring** is where the Spire's cleanliness gives way to something that resembles a place people live: narrow terraces, steep stairs, laundry strung between the buildings. The green-doored house of the second son of Vael stands here, and, by spring, a hand-lettered sign over its door.

**The upper rings** hold the great Houses, among them Ilvane, whose long gallery smells of beeswax and lilies and whose west-hall clock is one quarter-note flat because its master has never permitted it to be mended.

**The Hall of Canon** is the Synod's central hall: a vault so high the light arrives like weather, forty-one Custodians seated in a pale horseshoe, and, in the great open floor before them, a single lectern. It was built so that no one entering could forget they were small. For three generations it heard only from itself.

**The plaza** before the hall holds a wooden board with a slot for names. It is the newest thing in the Spire, and the only one anyone stands in the cold to read.

*Neither this account nor any before it has mapped the country whole. It notes only that the Spire's view, from its highest terrace, extends south to a low grey line at the edge of the sky, and that those who live there have, for the first time in three generations, begun to ask what it is.*
